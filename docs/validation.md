> Goal: **at least 10 real people** (target 15 survey responses + 5 interviews) by Friday evening, so that
> on Saturday we can say "we spoke to N parents, executors and young adults who lost a parent, and this is
> what they told us". "Validated users" is in the top business score band (27–30).

## 1. Interview script (10 questions, 10–15 minutes)

**Opening (read out):** "Thanks for your time. We're students building a tool for families at the
W3Node hackathon in Cape Town. I'd like to ask about your experience with money after a death in the
family. There are no right answers, you can skip any question, and we won't share your name without
your permission. Is it OK if I take notes?"

### A. Parents (35–55, with children)
1. How many children do you have, and what stage of school or study are they at?
2. If something happened to you tomorrow, how would their school or university fees be paid? Who would
   handle that money?
3. Do you have a will? (If yes: who is the executor? If no: what has stopped you?)
4. What do you have in place now: funeral cover, life cover, a burial society or stokvel, an education
   policy, savings, crypto? Which of these do you trust most to actually reach your children?
5. Have you seen a family wait for an estate to be wound up? How long did it take, and what did the
   family live on meanwhile?
6. What worries you most about money reaching your children: delays, relatives, the child spending it,
   fraud, costs, or something else?
7. Have you ever bought or held crypto or USDT (Tether)? Through which app? How would you feel about
   holding money for your children in digital dollars?
8. *(Now show the one-line idea.)* "Heirloom: you write your wishes with an AI assistant. The money sits
   in a locked digital-dollar vault. When you die, it only unlocks after people you chose (including
   your executor) confirm it, and then it pays school and university fees straight to the institution,
   within limits you set." What do you like? What would stop you using it?
9. Who would you name as your 3 trusted verifiers? Would you trust your burial society, your bank, your
   attorney or an exchange like Luno to offer this?
10. Would you pay **R99 a month** for this? If not, what would you pay, or would you rather it came free
    with your funeral cover? *(Note the exact amount they say.)*

### B. Young adults who lost a parent (18–30)
1. If you're comfortable: how old were you, and were you in school or studying at the time?
2. What happened with your fees in the year after? Did you pause, change institution or drop out?
3. How long did the estate take? Who controlled the money?
4. What did the family live on in the first 6 months?
5. Was any money lost, "borrowed" by relatives, or spent on something your parent wouldn't have wanted?
6. Was anything (a car, a house) sold for less than it was worth? Why?
7. If fees had been paid straight to the university from day one, what would have changed for you?
8. *(Show the one-line idea, as A8.)* What do you like? What worries you?
9. Who in your family would have been the right person to confirm the death and approve payments?
10. Can we quote you (first name or anonymously) in our pitch?

### C. Executor / attorney / fiduciary
1. How many deceased estates do you handle a year, and how long does a typical one take now?
2. Where are the biggest delays: the Master, banks, SARS, Home Affairs, the family?
3. How do families pay fees and living costs while the estate is open? Do you use the Master's
   living-expenses letter?
4. What do you charge, and how often is it the full 3.5% + VAT?
5. Have you seen fraud: fake death certificates, fake claims, assets sold to insiders?
6. If a client had a separate vault for education that unlocked once you, as executor, plus one other
   person confirmed the death, what problems would that create for you legally or practically?
7. Would that vault be part of the estate in your view, or would it need a trust structure?
8. *(Show the idea.)* Would you use a dashboard to verify deaths and approve payouts for your clients?
9. What would make you recommend it, and what would make you refuse?
10. Would you pay for it per firm, or should the family pay? Can we follow up after the hackathon?

**Closing (read out):** "Thank you. Can we send you the demo link on Saturday? And is there one other
person you think we should speak to?" *(Referrals are how you get to 10 fast.)*

## 3. Online survey (5 questions, Google Forms-ready)

**Form title:** Will your children's fees still get paid? (2 minutes)
**Description:** "A student hackathon project (W3Node, Cape Town). Anonymous. We only use the answers
to improve our project and to report totals in our pitch. Don't enter ID numbers or account details."

| # | Question | Google Forms type | Options |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which describes you best? | Multiple choice (required) | Parent with children under 25 · Young adult who lost a parent · Executor / attorney / fiduciary · Other |
| 2 | If you (or your parent) died, how long do you think it would take before money from the estate could pay school or university fees? | Multiple choice (required) | Under 1 month · 1–6 months · 6–12 months · More than a year · I don't know |
| 3 | What worries you most about money reaching the children after a death? (pick up to 2) | Checkboxes (required, max 2 via response validation) | Delays (estate frozen) · Relatives taking or misusing it · The child spending it on other things · Fraud / fake claims · Fees and costs · Assets sold too cheaply · Other |
| 4 | Would you use a service that locks money for your children's education and, after your death is confirmed by 2 of 3 people you choose, pays fees directly to the school or university? | Linear scale 1–5 (required) | 1 = definitely not … 5 = definitely yes |
| 5 | What would you pay for it? | Multiple choice (required) | Nothing, it should come free with funeral cover · Under R50/month · R50–R99/month · R100–R199/month · A once-off fee instead |

**Optional extra field (not counted in the 5):** "Leave your WhatsApp number if you're happy to have a
10-minute chat (optional)." Add the consent tick box: *"I agree that my answers may be used anonymously
in the Heirloom hackathon pitch."* (required).
