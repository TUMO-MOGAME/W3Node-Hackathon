## 3. Q&A prep (12 likely questions)

Answer in **under 20 seconds**, then stop. If you don't know, say so and say how you'd find out.

1. **"Is this legally valid? SA requires a paper will."**
   "Correct, and we don't replace it. The paper will stays the legal document; it names the executor and
   refers to the Heirloom estate. The executor is verifier number one. For real money we'll structure the
   vault as an inter vivos trust with a licensed trustee, and we're getting a legal opinion first."

2. **"What stops someone uploading a fake death certificate?"**
   "Four layers: the AI flags anomalies; two of three verifiers the parent chose, including the executor,
   must attest; then a waiting period runs during which the owner can veto with one click; and any
   verifier can freeze. A forged PDF alone moves nothing. Later we add a Home Affairs status check
   through a licensed ID bureau."

3. **"Why crypto at all? Why not a bank trust?"**
   "Three reasons. A bank trust is slow and expensive to set up for a R300,000 education fund. Rules in a
   program execute the same way every time and can be audited by the family. And a diaspora parent in
   Dubai can fund it in dollars in minutes. The family never needs to understand crypto: they see rand."

4. **"Why USD₮ specifically?"**
   "It's the most used dollar stablecoin, it's native on Solana with cheap fees, and South Africans already
   trade stablecoins (almost R80bn in the year to October 2025, per the SARB). A 10-year education plan in
   dollars also protects against rand depreciation. The program only accepts the official USD₮ mint."

5. **"Universities don't accept USDT."**
   "Right, and they won't need to. In the demo we pay a verified institution wallet. In production the vault
   pays a licensed off-ramp such as Luno Settlements or Stables, which pays the university in rand over
   PayShap or EFT with the student number as the reference. The university sees a normal rand payment."

6. **"What about regulation: FSCA, FICA, SARS?"**
   "Crypto assets are financial products, and 310 providers are FSCA-licensed. Heirloom is self-custodial
   software; buying, selling and rand payouts go through licensed CASPs that do FICA. SARS sees those
   flows under CARF from March 2026, and we give the executor a full export for tax. We'll confirm with
   counsel whether we need our own licence."

7. **"How do you make money?"**
   "R99 a month while the parent is alive, 1% of each payout capped per year, and 1.5% on asset sales. That's
   about R8,000 over a typical family's lifetime, against up to R12,000 of executor fees on the same R300k.
   B2B2C: R15–R25 per member a month as an add-on through burial societies, insurers and exchanges."

8. **"Why Tether WDK and not Phantom?"**
   "WDK gives us self-custodial wallets inside our app for every role: parents, children, verifiers,
   buyers and the AI agent. Nobody needs to install a browser wallet, and the gasless module means families
   don't need SOL. Heirloom never holds anyone's keys."

9. **"What if the AI is wrong or gets tricked?"**
   "The AI only recommends. Caps, floors and quorums are in the Solana program, so even a fully
   compromised agent can't pay more than the cap, pay a non-approved payee, or sell below the floor. You saw
   the contract reject the overclaim. Document text is treated as data, and humans approve exceptions."

10. **"Privacy and POPIA? Death certificates on a blockchain?"**
    "Never. Documents stay off-chain; only a SHA-256 hash goes on-chain, so we can prove a document wasn't
    changed without revealing it. Encryption, a privacy policy and consent at onboarding are in the launch plan."

11. **"Does this scale beyond South Africa?" / "Who's the competition?"**
    "Sub-Saharan Africa received $205bn on-chain last year, and Nigeria and Kenya lead in stablecoin use.
    The rules engine is country-neutral; only the off-ramp and legal templates change. Competitors are
    either traditional (Capital Legacy, Sanlam Trust, banks: slow, lump sums, no crypto) or crypto
    dead-man's switches (Casa, SolWill: they hand the heir everything). Nobody pays the purpose, with a
    human quorum, for African families."

12. **"What's real and what's simulated?"**
    "Real: the Anchor program on devnet, the WDK wallets, the AI agent, and the cap, floor and quorum checks,
    which are tested. Simulated: devnet uses our own 6-decimal test USD₮ mint, institutions are demo wallets,
    the waiting period is 2 minutes instead of weeks, documents are samples, and the rand off-ramp isn't
    built yet. [If done: we also made one real mainnet USD₮ transfer with a WDK wallet, shown in the video.]
    The contract isn't audited."
